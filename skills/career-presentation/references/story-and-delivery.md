# Story and delivery

Suggested five-minute structure, adjustable to the brief:

| Slide | Audience question | Approximate speaking time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | What relevant experience do you bring? | 30 seconds |
| Role fit | Which needs can you demonstrate? | 45 seconds |
| Case: challenge and contribution | What did you actually do? | 90 seconds |
| Case: outcome and learning | What happened and what did you learn? | 60 seconds |
| Close | How does this experience relate to our role? | 45 seconds |

These 270 seconds leave 30 seconds for transitions. If questions must fit within five minutes, shorten the story further. Never treat these timings as a measured rehearsal.

For a three-minute introduction, use three slides: relevant background, one evidence-rich example, and role connection. For a longer portfolio interview, expand the cases rather than adding a long biography. A student can use coursework or volunteering with the correct labels.

Speaker notes should be natural spoken language, not an essay pasted onto a slide. Keep one short opening sentence, two or three supporting cues, and a transition. Match the user's speaking comfort; do not write an advanced English script that they cannot reasonably use. Translation support does not establish fluency.

Use real artifacts when authorized, simple process diagrams when useful, and numerical charts only for supplied data. Avoid skill-rating bars, decorative metrics, and generic headshots. Choose readable text and sufficient contrast; reduce content instead of shrinking type. Confidential backup material does not belong in hidden slides.
