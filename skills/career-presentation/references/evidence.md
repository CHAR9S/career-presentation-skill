# Evidence rules

Use a requirement-to-evidence table before choosing cases. Carry facts forward from a resume without increasing their strength.

| Source fact | Safe presentation | Unsupported upgrade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trained two colleagues on a tracker | Explain the training and tracker | Managed a team of two |
| Organized folders by client and month | Show an anonymized structure if authorized | Saved 40% of retrieval time |
| Team delivered a campaign; candidate animated titles | Identify team outcome and personal animation work separately | Owned the entire campaign |
| Prototype, not launched | Label as prototype and explain decisions | Successful commercial launch |

Ask what the applicant did, for whom, with which tools, under what constraints, and what changed. Ask for a relevant result, but do not require a number. If the source is an estimate, retain that qualification. If a screenshot is unavailable, a clearly labelled conceptual diagram may explain a process; it cannot be passed off as a real artifact.

Do not put SINs, passport details, home addresses, private correspondence, or confidential project data into slides or notes. Public portfolio reuse still requires checking the user's right to share the assets. An anonymized project must retain the true scope and contribution.
